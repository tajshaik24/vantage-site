How Vantage works

The short version, for the curious

It talks to the Screen Sharing already on your Mac

Every Mac has Screen Sharing built in. Vantage speaks the same protocol Apple's own Screen Sharing app uses, so there's nothing to install on the Mac: turn Screen Sharing on, sign in with your Mac account, and you're in.

iPad or iPhone→your network or Tailscale→macOS Screen Sharing
  • Sign-in uses the Mac's own secure login, and the session is encrypted the way Screen Sharing encrypts it.
  • The picture arrives in Apple's Screen Sharing format, pushed by the Mac as it changes. The pointer is drawn on your device, so it moves the moment your finger does.
  • Linux and Windows computers running a VNC server work too, with secure sign-in.

Anywhere, with Tailscale built in

Vantage runs its own Tailscale connection inside the app. Sign in once in Settings and your Macs on your tailnet show up, reachable from any network, without a separate VPN app on your iPad or iPhone. Each saved computer is either Local or Tailscale, so you always know which way you're connected.

Standard and High Performance

  • Standard works everywhere and keeps your typing and clicks first: the picture can sharpen a moment later, but input never waits behind it.
  • High Performance (M-series iPad, Apple silicon Mac) asks the Mac for the same stream Apple's High Performance mode uses: HEVC video at 60 frames a second with the Mac's sound, encrypted end to end. It needs a fast network; if the connection can't keep up, Vantage switches to Standard by itself and tells you.

Remote: controls without the screen

Keyboard & Mouse, Presentation and Controller connect to your Mac the same way, but ask for only one picture (to learn the screen size and where the pointer is) and then stop the video. What's left is your input, which is tiny, so a remote starts fast and barely touches your battery or data.

  • Keyboard & Mouse: a trackpad, a Mac keyboard and dictation.
  • Presentation: slide controls for Keynote, PowerPoint and Google Slides, and a laser pointer you aim by tilting your phone. In PowerPoint it switches on PowerPoint's own laser.
  • Controller: on-screen or Bluetooth, with each button sending the key you choose.

Laptop Mode on iPhone Duo

Half-open your iPhone Duo with the hinge across, like a laptop, and choose Laptop Mode. Your Mac goes above the hinge, shaped to fit it, and a Mac keyboard and trackpad go below. Close the phone and the session waits for you.

The Vantage Helper Coming soon

Everything above works with Screen Sharing alone. The Vantage Helper is a small, optional menu-bar app for your Mac that adds what Screen Sharing can't carry:

  • Fuller remotes. Keyboard & Mouse types real Mac keys, scrolls like a trackpad and gets volume, play/pause, brightness and Mission Control keys. Presentation's laser becomes a red dot or a spotlight drawn over any app, and Start brings Keynote or PowerPoint to the front first. The helper asks your Mac for Accessibility access to do this.
  • A real game controller. Controller shows up on the Mac as a game controller, sticks and triggers included, instead of pressing keys.
  • Native multi-finger touch. Your fingers reach the Mac as a real touchscreen, the way the new touch MacBook Pro works. It's waiting on macOS 27.3.

The game controller and touch also need a permission from Apple that the helper doesn't have yet.

  • Pairing: your device and your Mac each show a six-digit code, and you approve on both. The Mac only listens for pairing for two minutes after you ask it to.
  • Every connection after that proves who's on each end and is encrypted, so nobody else on the network can send your Mac input or read what's sent.

Private by design

Vantage connects straight to your own computers. Passwords stay in your device's keychain. There are no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking. See the privacy policy.
